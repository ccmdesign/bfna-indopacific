## Overall Introduction

In recent decades, the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) has risen dramatically in geopolitical significance. Its 11 member states play increasingly crucial roles in the global economy, and the region has emerged as a leading producer of many of the world's most critical raw materials. Not only are members such as Indonesia central to nickel production, but the region as a whole is rich in tin, copper, bauxite and rare earth elements (REEs). As a result, ASEAN has become increasingly vital to supply chains supporting high-tech industries. Consequently, for the United States, the European Union and the People's Republic of China, understanding the complexities of ASEAN is more important than ever before. This interactive map explores the ASEAN member states and their economic and strategic ties with external powers. Select a country to learn more.

## Note on the importance of critical minerals (legend annotation)

Myanmar and Vietnam are emerging REE suppliers. Laos is the second-largest producer of bismuth. And Malaysia is one of the few countries globally with REE processing capacity outside China.

## Indonesia

**Description**
Indonesia is the most populous country in Southeast Asia, with 287 million people spread across 17,000 islands. It is the largest economy in ASEAN and maintains a longstanding doctrine of *bebas aktif* ("free and active") foreign policy, refusing to side with any great power bloc. Indonesia hedges by trading predominantly with China while anchoring its security architecture to the U.S. and expanding its relationship with the EU.

**Trade**
China is Indonesia's largest single trade partner, with bilateral trade reaching a record $135.15 billion in 2024. In comparison, U.S. goods trade totalled an estimated $45.8 billion in 2025, while EU trade in goods amounted to €27.3 billion ($31.9 billion) in 2024. Notably, the top source of foreign direct investment in 2025 was Singapore at $17.4 billion, reflecting Indonesia's deep integration into intra-ASEAN capital flows alongside its broader great-power relationships.

**Critical Minerals**
In 2024, Indonesia was the world's largest producer of mined nickel, accounting for 62% of global output. It was also the second-largest producer of mined tin (19%) and cobalt (12%), as well as the sixth-largest producer of natural zeolites (10%) and zirconium mineral concentrates (6%). Other mineral commodities produced in the country include bauxite, copper, ferroalloys and gold. These vast critical mineral reserves, combined with Indonesia's geographic position, make it essential to the global clean energy transition and one of the most strategically consequential countries in the Indo-Pacific.

**Key Facts**
- GDP growth rate (2026): 5%
- GDP per capita, PPP (2024): $16,448.3
- Total trade as % of GDP (2024): 43%
- FDI net inflows (2024): $24.28 billion

**Trade Agreements**
- EU: ASEAN–EEC Cooperation Agreement (1980), EU–Indonesia Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) – FTA (signed 2025)
- US: US–ASEAN TIFA (2006)
- China: ASEAN–China Free Trade Area (2009), Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (2021), several MoUs

## Thailand

**Description**
Thailand is an upper-middle-income country and ASEAN's second-largest economy, growing by 2.5% in 2024. The kingdom serves as a land bridge linking South Asia, Indochina and maritime Southeast Asia. A U.S. treaty ally, Thailand conducts regular joint exercises with Washington while also holding annual military exercises with China since 2005. Although this reflects its hedging strategy, recent trends suggest a gradual economic and military drift toward Beijing.

**Trade**
Thailand's economy is modern, industrialized and export-oriented, driven by services, manufacturing and agriculture, with China as its largest trading partner in 2025. The kingdom also maintains deep trade ties with the U.S., ranking among its top 10 trading partners. The EU is Thailand's fourth-largest trading partner, accounting for 7.2% of its goods trade in 2025, while Thailand ranks as the EU's 25th-largest partner, with bilateral goods trade totaling €44.3 billion.

**Critical Minerals**
Thailand is rich in elements such as tin and tungsten, which are essential for key industries like electric vehicle production, battery storage, and renewable energy technologies. In 2025, the kingdom signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the U.S. to strengthen cooperation on critical minerals supply chain development and expansion.

**Key Facts**
- GDP growth rate (2026): 1.5%
- GDP per capita, PPP (2024): $24,712.1
- Total trade as % of GDP (2024): 137%
- FDI net inflows (2024): $14.3 billion

**Trade Agreements**
- EU: ASEAN–EEC Cooperation Agreement (1980), EU–Thailand FTA (ongoing)
- US: US–ASEAN TIFA (2006)
- China: ASEAN–China Free Trade Area (2009), Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (2021), several MoUs

## Singapore

**Description**
Singapore is one of the world's great financial centers. Leveraging a crucial position at the mouth of the busy Strait of Malacca, the tiny city-state exercises disproportionate influence on the global economy. Its ports serve as vital transshipment hubs for the ASEAN region, while its banks and trading floors hold billions in U.S., European and Chinese assets. Though Singapore produces relatively little on its own, its geopolitical position, advanced infrastructure and reputation for business-friendly governance have propelled it to the top of global economic rankings.

**Trade**
More than almost any other nation, Singapore relies on international trade. The city-state has maintained a long and lucrative trading relationship with China, with bilateral trade between the two accounting for upwards of $70 billion in 2025. Despite its strong ties with China, Singapore remains a crucial trading partner with both the U.S. and EU. In 2025, Singapore was the EU's 21st-largest trading partner worldwide for trade in goods and the largest Asian investor in the EU.

**Critical Minerals**
Given its small size, Singapore has essentially zero mining capacity. Nevertheless, the city-state has carved out an essential role in the global critical mineral supply chain. Resources mined throughout the region flow through its ports in vast quantities, allowing the country to become a clearing house for a wide range of ores and processed goods. Large proportions of these resources flow onward to destinations in China, the U.S. and Europe, where they are refined or incorporated into various high-tech products. Singapore also imports significant quantities of critical minerals to supply its own industries.

**Key Facts**
- GDP growth rate (2026): 3.5%
- GDP per capita, PPP (2024): $150,689.3
- Total trade as % of GDP (2024): 322%
- FDI net inflows (2024): $135.08 billion

**Trade Agreements**
- EU: ASEAN–EEC Cooperation Agreement (1980), EU–Singapore FTA (2019), EU–Singapore Investment Protection Agreement (2019), EU–Singapore Digital Trade Agreement (2026)
- US: US–Singapore FTA (2004), US–ASEAN TIFA (2006)
- China: ASEAN–China Free Trade Area (2009), Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (2021), several MoUs

## Malaysia

**Description**
One of the region's most dynamic economies, Malaysia has vaulted up global economic rankings in recent years. As of 2026, it ranks as the world's 34th-largest economy by nominal GDP and 28th-largest by purchasing power parity. The country has benefited from steadily increasing foreign investment, particularly as U.S. technology firms have sought to diversify away from China and Taiwan. Malaysia has also carved out a key role in the critical minerals supply chain, both as a source of raw materials and as a refiner of unprocessed ores.

**Trade**
Located astride several of the world's most important trade routes, Malaysia has historically relied heavily on trade. Its economy has consistently ranked among the most trade-dependent globally. Malaysia serves as a key link in a variety of global supply chains, particularly for high-tech goods like semiconductors and integrated circuits. While the U.S. and Europe have significant trade ties with Kuala Lumpur, they lag far behind China, which has long enjoyed a dominant trade relationship with Malaysia. In 2024, bilateral trade between China and Malaysia exceeded $154 billion.

**Critical Minerals**
While it lacks the massive resource stockpiles of its neighbor Indonesia, Malaysia has nevertheless emerged as a key actor in the global critical mineral economy. Malaysian mines produce considerable volumes of bauxite, aluminum and nickel, the majority of which flows into Chinese markets. In recent years, however, Malaysia has sought to shift away from intensive mining operations by expanding its role as a refiner and processor. In 2025, the Australian firm Lynas opened the first refinery capable of processing heavy rare earths outside of China in Malaysia.

**Key Facts**
- GDP growth rate (2026): 4.7%
- GDP per capita, PPP (2024): $38,779.3
- Total trade as % of GDP (2024): 137%
- FDI net inflows: $15.59 billion (2022 — confirm a 2024 figure)

**Trade Agreements**
- EU: ASEAN–EEC Cooperation Agreement (1980), EU–Malaysia Partnership and Cooperation Agreement (PCA) (signed 2022), Malaysia–EU Free Trade Agreement (MEUFTA) – proposed FTA
- US: US–ASEAN TIFA (2006)
- China: ASEAN–China Free Trade Area (2009), Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (2021), several MoUs

## Vietnam

**Description**
Vietnam is one of the region's fastest-growing manufacturing hubs and has emerged as an alternative to China in global supply chains. As of 2025, it ranked 23rd globally by purchasing power parity with a recorded GDP growth of 7.1%. Vietnam shares a 1,297 km land border with China and has experienced repeated tensions over contested waters in the South China Sea. Notably, Vietnam maintains Comprehensive Strategic Partnerships with both China and the United States, reflecting a carefully calibrated hedging strategy. Rather than aligning firmly with either side, Vietnam seeks to maximize leverage among major powers.

**Trade**
Vietnam is highly dependent on international trade, with its key exports consisting of electrical machinery, apparel, textiles and footwear. As of 2025, China is Vietnam's largest single trade partner, with bilateral trade reaching a record $296 billion. However, Vietnam's trade relationships are notably diversified: in 2025, goods trade totaled an estimated $209.5 billion with the U.S., making it its 8th-largest trade partner. Moreover, with €76 billion of bilateral trade in goods, Vietnam is the EU's largest trading partner within ASEAN.

**Critical Minerals**
The country holds an estimated 3.5 million metric tons of rare earth reserves, ranking sixth globally. It is particularly rich in rare earth elements, bauxite, tungsten, titanium and manganese. It is the world's second-largest producer of tungsten (4.1% of global output), third in cement (2.3%) and fifth in fluorspar (1.4%). Beyond these, Vietnam produces a wide range of mineral commodities, including aluminum, antimony, bismuth, cobalt and graphite, among many others.

- GDP per capita, PPP (2024): $16,385.5
- Total trade as % of GDP (2024): 174%

**Trade Agreements**

## Philippines

**Description**
Despite its relatively small size and challenging geography, the Philippines has built one of the region's most vibrant economies. Led by a thriving services sector, the archipelagic nation currently ranks as the world's 35th-largest economy by nominal GDP. While Manila maintains extensive diplomatic and military ties with the United States, it has developed strong economic relations with a variety of regional partners, most notably China. With rapidly developing infrastructure and annual growth rates regularly exceeding 5%, the Philippines is poised to become a regional economic leader. However, corruption and inequality remain pressing concerns.

**Trade**
Much of the Philippines' economic success in recent years has been fueled by international trade. While a large portion (approximately 18% in April 2026) of its exports flow to the United States, the country is highly dependent on China for imports, with upwards of 29% of total imports originating in the PRC. Manila has also expanded its trade ties with the European Union in recent years, with bilateral trade in goods amounting to €17.6 billion in 2025. Key exports include semiconductors, integrated circuits and critical minerals.

**Critical Minerals**
Despite its small size, the Philippines plays a growing role in international critical mineral supply chains. The island nation contains vast — and largely untapped — reserves of key commodities such as nickel, copper and cobalt. The former is of particular significance, as the Philippines has emerged as one of the world's leading nickel exporters, with reserves estimated to exceed $175 billion. Most of these exports come in the form of raw nickel ore, the vast majority of which flows to China. However, the Philippine government has recently debated imposing a ban on raw nickel ore exports, with the aim of expanding refining capabilities and moving up the value chain.

**Key Facts**
- GDP growth rate (2026): 4.1%
- GDP per capita, PPP (2024): $11,794.1
- Total trade as % of GDP (2024): 66%
- FDI net inflows (2024): $9.4 billion

**Trade Agreements**
- EU: ASEAN–EEC Cooperation Agreement (1980), EU–Philippines FTA (ongoing negotiations), GSP+ (2014)
- US: US–ASEAN TIFA (2006); Mutual Defense Treaty (1951)
- China: ASEAN–China Free Trade Area (2009), Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (2021), several MoUs

## Brunei

**Description**
Despite its small size and population, Brunei is an economic powerhouse. Among Southeast Asian nations, it lags behind only Singapore in Human Development Index, ranking 60th globally. Brunei's economic success has been driven almost entirely by its sizable reserves of crude oil and natural gas, exports of which account for over half of GDP. In recent years, the Bruneian government has sought to diversify its economy away from the oil and gas industry. In 2007, Brunei launched the Wawasan Brunei 2035 national development plan, which aims to prioritize sectors such as mining and financial services.

**Trade**
With a small internal market, Brunei is exceedingly dependent on international trade. Exports of oil and natural gas account for 80% of the nation's total exports and 53.5% of its total GDP. The majority of these exports remain in the Indo-Pacific, with Australia, China and Japan purchasing a significant portion. Beyond oil and gas, Brunei relies heavily on foreign imports to sustain its economy and population. While fellow ASEAN states account for most of these imports, China has also expanded its footprint in Brunei in recent years. The massive Hengyi Industries petrochemical plant, developed as a partnership between a Chinese firm and the Bruneian government, now accounts for upwards of 10% of Brunei's total exports.

**Critical Minerals**
With extremely limited landmass and an economy oriented almost exclusively around oil and gas, Brunei's current role in the critical mineral industry is minimal. However, the government has recently begun taking steps to diversify its energy portfolio, including exploring new sources of critical minerals. While mining is unlikely to expand significantly, the country is nevertheless poised to become an important logistical hub in the regional critical mineral supply chain.

**Key Facts**
- GDP growth rate (2026): 2.6%
- GDP per capita, PPP (2024): $89,879.4
- Total trade as % of GDP (2024): 133%
- FDI net inflows (2024): ~$29 million

**Trade Agreements**
- EU: ASEAN–EEC Cooperation Agreement (1980)
- US: US–ASEAN TIFA (2006)
- China: ASEAN–China Free Trade Area (2009), Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (2021), several MoUs

## Cambodia

**Description**
Cambodia, one of ASEAN's smallest nations, has recovered from the COVID-19 pandemic and is currently experiencing real GDP growth of around 6%. The kingdom borders Thailand, Vietnam and Laos, and is strategically located with direct access to the Gulf of Thailand through the Ream Naval Base. China, Cambodia's primary military partner, has secured a strategic foothold at the base, cementing close bilateral ties. While Cambodia leans strongly toward China, it continues to engage with both the United States and the European Union.

**Trade**
Cambodia's key sectors include garments, agriculture and a recovering tourism industry. The United States remains Cambodia's largest single-country export destination, accounting for approximately 38% of total exports, primarily consisting of garments, footwear and travel goods. U.S. goods trade with Cambodia totaled an estimated $15.7 billion in 2025, while EU–Cambodia trade amounted to €4.3 billion in 2024. By comparison, in 2025, China was Cambodia's largest overall trading partner, with bilateral trade reaching a record $19.73 billion. Moreover, it accounts for 53% of approved foreign direct investment ($3.4 billion in 2024).

**Critical Minerals**
While Cambodia produces gold, clay, copper, gemstones, iron ore and other minerals, the critical minerals sector remains largely underdeveloped. Currently, mineral production contributes only 3% to the country's GDP. Other mineral commodities produced in Cambodia include limestone, salt, sand and gravel, steel and stone.

**Key Facts**
- GDP growth rate (2026): ~6%
- GDP per capita, PPP (2024): $7,966.9
- Total trade as % of GDP (2024): 143%
- FDI net inflows (2024): $4.39 billion

**Trade Agreements**
- EU: ASEAN–EEC Cooperation Agreement (1980), EU–Cambodia Cooperation Agreement (1999), Everything But Arms (2001)
- US: US–ASEAN TIFA (2006)
- China: ASEAN–China Free Trade Area (2009), Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (2021), China–Cambodia Free Trade Agreement (2022), several MoUs

## Laos

**Description**
While relatively small in size and population (8 million), Laos serves as a critical transit corridor. It is the only landlocked country in Southeast Asia, bordered by China, Vietnam, Myanmar, Thailand and Cambodia. In 2024, its GDP grew by 4.1%, reflecting steady growth; however, the country is deeply dependent on China, particularly for its infrastructure needs. As of 2026, a Chinese state-owned firm holds a 90% stake and a 25-year concession over Laos's national electricity grid, while the $6 billion China–Laos Railway gives Beijing a controlling stake in its operation, making it the ASEAN member most structurally dependent on Beijing.

**Trade**
China is Laos's dominant trade and investment partner by a commanding margin, with bilateral trade reaching approximately $6.29 billion in 2024. In comparison, U.S. goods trade totalled an estimated $2.1 billion in 2025, while EU trade in goods amounted to around €500 million. China's dominance extends beyond trade: Beijing owns roughly half of Laos's $10.5 billion foreign debt, creating significant dependencies.

**Critical Minerals**
In 2024, Laos was the fourth-largest producer of refined bismuth, accounting for 3.0% of global output, as well as the sixth-largest producer of potash, with 5.1%. As it continues to develop its critical minerals sector — particularly in copper, tin and antimony — its importance is rapidly growing.

## Myanmar

**Description**
One of the world's most ethnically diverse nations, Myanmar has been embroiled in a nearly constant civil war since gaining independence in 1948. Riven by internal divisions and destabilized by multiple military coups, the country has struggled to build a modern economic system. Nevertheless, Myanmar's large population and abundant natural resources have allowed for sporadic periods of rapid economic growth. Starting in 2011, the country embarked on a series of reforms aimed at liberalizing the economy, resulting in significant growth. However, these gains were largely reversed following the 2021 coup, as widespread violence and government mismanagement have resulted in spiraling inflation and poverty.

**Trade**
Despite its position along some of the world's busiest maritime trade routes, Myanmar conducts the vast majority of its international trade via overland routes. Since 2021, the ruling junta has been isolated from many potential trade partners, including the U.S., which suspended its bilateral Trade and Investment Framework Agreement in March of that year. As a result, the government has expanded its trade ties with neighboring China, with bilateral trade between the two reaching $19.4 billion in 2025.

**Critical Minerals**
Myanmar is among the world's richest sources of heavy rare earth elements such as dysprosium and terbium, which play essential roles in the manufacturing of advanced batteries. As part of its decades-long effort to dominate the critical mineral industry, China has invested heavily in extracting these resources, often causing significant damage to local ecosystems. While the U.S. and Europe have expressed interest in following China's example in Myanmar, the country's instability and unpredictability have made significant in-roads challenging.

**Key Facts**
- GDP growth rate (2026): 3%
- GDP per capita, PPP (2024): $5,997.5
- Total trade as % of GDP: not available
- FDI net inflows (2024): ~$1.10 billion

**Trade Agreements**
- EU: Everything But Arms
- US: US–ASEAN TIFA (2006) — **SUSPENDED** (March 2021)
- China: ASEAN–China Free Trade Area (2009), Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (2021), several MoUs

## Timor-Leste

> Out of scope for the current build (no geo feature / tier entry yet) — included here for completeness. See follow-up.

**Description**
One of the world's youngest nations, Timor-Leste possesses a relatively small, developing economy. According to United Nations criteria, it is listed as a Least Developed Country due to its high levels of poverty, illiteracy and economic vulnerability. Since regaining independence from Indonesia in 2002, the small island country has seen modest economic growth fueled by government spending, foreign direct investment and a growing petrochemicals industry. While it boasts one of the world's youngest populations, further growth has been hampered by insufficient infrastructure, lagging job creation and a decentralized, heavily rural populace.

**Trade**
Timor-Leste relies heavily on international trade to support its population and fuel economic growth. Like several other ASEAN states, its exports are dominated by crude oil, much of which flows to Thailand and the U.S. With relatively limited domestic manufacturing, the country is highly dependent on imported goods, including refined oil and rice. China accounts for a significant proportion of these imports, with Timor-Leste importing approximately $266 million in goods and services from China in 2024. While bilateral trade with the EU remains limited (€17 million in 2025), the two sides have signed several agreements aimed at boosting trade and spurring investment.

**Critical Minerals**
While Timor-Leste's involvement in the critical mineral supply chain is currently minimal, it is taking steps to increase its footprint. The country is believed to possess significant reserves of key minerals such as nickel, copper and manganese, the vast majority of which remain untapped. Since 2020, several foreign firms have invested in mining operations in Timor-Leste, including Australia's Estrella Resources, which has secured rights to mine for manganese in the country's northern regions. These investments have been welcomed by the Timorese government, which is keen to diversify its economy beyond oil and gas.

**Key Facts**
- GDP growth rate (2026): 4.1%
- GDP per capita, PPP (2024): $4,422.8
- Total trade as % of GDP (2024): 95%
- FDI net inflows (2024): $225.47 million
